The plan and direction are right — but two things will quietly break staging if they carry over from prod by default, and "everything else same as prod" is exactly where they'd slip through.

**Staging must have its own database — separate from prod.** This is the make-or-break. If the staging deploy inherits prod's `DATABASE_URL`, then its auto-seed writes the test accounts into the *prod* DB, and journey-1's checkout mutations land in *prod* data — you've recreated the exact problem you're purging, just at a new URL. When you create the staging deployment, give it its own Postgres instance / its own `DATABASE_URL`. Same Stripe test keys, same `ENVIRONMENT`≠`PROD`, but a *distinct* database. A staging deploy pointed at prod's DB is worse than no staging at all, because it looks isolated and isn't.

**Confirm staging auto-seeds the catalog, not just the accounts.** Login working proves the five accounts seeded — it does not prove there's anything bookable to put in a cart. Journey-1 is add-to-cart → checkout, and you already saw Journey 1B skip when no services were seeded. If staging's startup seed creates accounts but no Kyoto services/inventory, the cart can't be filled and journey-1 either skips or reds on an empty cart — neither is the real result you're after. So the agent's post-staging probe needs a third check beyond health and login: that a bookable service exists in the seeded market. If the startup seed doesn't include the catalog, that's the fix before any run.

Those two are blocking. The prod purge has two upgrades that aren't blocking the run but are worth doing while you're in there:

**Make the purge pattern-based, not a hardcoded five-email list.** Your test-account convention is `{market}-{specialty}@traveloure.test` across five markets — that's potentially ~45 accounts, not five. A hardcoded list of five misses the rest if any of them reached prod. Purge `WHERE email LIKE '%@traveloure.test'` instead — `.test` is a reserved TLD that can never belong to a real user, so the pattern is safe and it catches every test account, present and future.

**Add a guard to the seed script itself.** The startup purge is a janitor cleaning up after the fact — it removes test accounts on the next prod boot, but they still got in via a manual seed that bypassed the env-gate, and nothing stops that from happening again. Put a refusal in the seed script: if `ENVIRONMENT === 'PROD'` (or the target is the prod DB), it errors out instead of seeding. That's the lock; the purge is the mop. Together you get defense in depth — the seed can't target prod, and anything that somehow slips in gets purged on restart.

So the order:
- **You:** create staging with its **own database**, test Stripe keys, `ENVIRONMENT`≠`PROD`; redeploy prod (ships the purge + `db:true`).
- **Replit agent:** confirm staging health (`db:true`), login (accounts seeded), **and a bookable service exists** (catalog seeded); upgrade the purge to the `@traveloure.test` pattern; add the prod-refusal guard to the seed script; set `E2E_BASE_URL` to staging.
- **Claude Code:** Model B restructure, point at staging, run journey-1.

The staging-needs-its-own-DB point is the one I'd double-check before you click create — it's the difference between staging doing its job and staging being prod wearing a different hostname.
