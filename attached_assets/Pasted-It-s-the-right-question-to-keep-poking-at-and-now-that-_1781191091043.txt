It's the right question to keep poking at — and now that the redesign made the summary look like Stage B's header grown down, the "why have both" feeling is sharper. But the answer isn't to merge them, because they do genuinely different jobs:
Stage A (summary) is a cross-trip dashboard item — its real job is triage across plans: scan "Your active plans," see which is SOON, which has a pending expert request, which has action items, and pick one. Stage B is single-trip deep control — the map, transport, booking, the whole working surface for one trip. They look related now (by design), but one is a list item and one is the page. That's why "make Stage B the summary page" isn't a clean swap: a list of full control centers is unusable. If you have three trips, you can't render three maps + transport sections + upsell slots stacked in a dashboard — you need the compact summary to choose between them. Stage B literally can't be the list.
So the real hinge is: how many active plans does a typical user have at once?
If it's usually one — which for most travelers it is, you're planning or on a single trip — then you're right, and the resolution is exactly your instinct: skip the summary and land directly on Stage B when there's one active plan, and only show the summary list when there are two or more. The summary card stops being a redundant smaller-version-of-the-thing and goes back to being what it's for: a chooser, shown only when there's actually something to choose between.
If users often have several concurrent plans, then the summary list earns its place as the triage dashboard, and the extra tap is the cost of having a list — standard list→detail.
My read: don't literally make Stage B the page (that breaks the multi-trip case and the "Your active plans" dashboard, which is also your nav hub and new-trip entry point), but do collapse the extra step where it's redundant — auto-open Stage B for the single-active-plan case, reserve the summary list for 2+. That gives you the no-extra-step feel you keep reaching for, without losing the dashboard when it's genuinely needed. And it's a small routing/conditional change, not a redesign — and nothing you just built is wasted: the summary card still exists for the multi-trip case, and the "grown up" continuity is exactly what makes tapping from the list into a trip feel seamless.
So the question that decides it: does a user typically have one active plan, or several? If one, the answer to "why not make Stage B the page" is basically "yes, for that case — auto-open it, and only fall back to the summary list when there's more than one."
