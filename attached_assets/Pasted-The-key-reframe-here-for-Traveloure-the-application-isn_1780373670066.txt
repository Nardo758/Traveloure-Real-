The key reframe here: for Traveloure, the application isn't a profile form — it's a knowledge-extraction instrument. The local knowledge is the product, so the application should double as (1) a vetting gate and (2) the first deposit into a structured, machine-readable knowledge base that Gronk and the matching engine can actually query. Most marketplaces fail here by capturing a bio paragraph, which is useless to an AI and indistinguishable from a tourist's.
Here's how I'd structure it.
Split it into two layers
Don't try to capture everything at application time — experts abandon long forms. Use a short application to vet + seed, then capture the bulk progressively after acceptance (gamified, with the AI Content Assistant drafting from their answers). This mirrors the profile-completion follow-up cadence already in your playbook.
Application stage (short, ~5–8 min) — vet and seed

Identity & locality: market, specific neighborhoods of deep familiarity (multi-select, not "Tokyo" but "Shimokitazawa, Kōenji"), years lived there, languages, "how are you local" (born/raised/lived 10yr/etc.).
Knowledge proof — this is your differentiator. Instead of "describe your expertise," ask 2–3 specific questions only a real local can answer and that are hard to fake from a guidebook. e.g. "A traveler wants the best ramen near Shibuya — where do you send them, where do you steer them away from, and why?" This simultaneously vets credibility and captures your first consultative knowledge nuggets.
Specialties as structured tags (food, safety/navigation, cultural interpretation, nightlife, family, LGBTQ+, budget, luxury, photography), plus traveler types served. These feed AI matching directly.

Ongoing knowledge capture — the actual asset
The unit to capture is not prose but the knowledge nugget: an atomic, attributable, queryable record. Schema something like:

type: tip / warning / recommendation / cultural-note / hidden-cost
entity: linked POI, neighborhood, or "general"
insight: the actual knowledge
who_its_for / who_its_NOT_for
seasonality / confidence
expert_id (powers your "via ExpertName" attribution)

This structure is what makes the consultative positioning real — it forces capture of what's wrong with an option and hidden costs, not just "this place is great." It's the difference between an expert and a listing. And because each nugget is tied to an entity and an expert, Gronk can blend it with SERP/native inventory and surface it with attribution.
One design principle to enforce: every free-text answer should resolve to structured fields the engine can use. If an answer can't be tagged to an entity, a traveler type, or a specialty, it doesn't earn its place in the matching layer — it's just a bio.
