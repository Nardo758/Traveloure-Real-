But don't let the summary's "authorization concern? None" close the platform-expert finding. The agent flagged it honestly and then filed it under "A7's problem, not transport's." The attribution is correct; the dismissal isn't. Here's the actual shape of it:
getTripRole falls back to user.role when there's no collaborator row, so any account with platform role expert or admin gets canMutateTrip = true on every trip on the platform — including trips they have no assigned relationship to. That contradicts your own spec, which is assigned expert edit rights, not any-expert-edits-anything. And the blast radius isn't transport — it's all 8 call sites now, including these two new ones. For a marketplace that will onboard experts at scale, "any expert can read and mutate any traveler's itinerary" is a real privacy-and-integrity hole, not a backward-compat footnote.
So: transport ships. The getTripRole fallback goes on the pre-launch security list, same bucket as the password-reset blocker — it's a known, tracked decision, not something that rides in unexamined because a summary said "None." The fix lives in A7, not transport, and it's narrow: either require a trip_collaborators row for everyone (drop the user.role fallback), or restrict the fallback to admin only, or scope an expert's "expert" role to trips they're actually assigned to. Pre-launch with a hand-picked expert pool the risk is contained, but it shouldn't survive to an open marketplace.
