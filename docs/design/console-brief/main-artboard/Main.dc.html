The AI Concierge on the slip, and the console around it

The AI Concierge should not be a place. It is an actor on the plan, and the slip is the plan, so every AI surface becomes a control on the slip that reads the plan live and writes only through proposals. The AI Planner splits at the mint: before a plan exists it is a door into the one planning modal, after the mint it is a drawer on that plan's slip. The console stops being ten peer destinations and becomes one spine, My plans, and every other tab becomes a view that reads plans and lands its actions on one plan's slip.

Rulings this brief asks for
1A nullable trip id on the AI conversation row, so a conversation can belong to a plan. Additive, no check, declared in the schema file.
2The concierge page becomes a door into the one modal, on the quick-start retirement pattern. Its guest claim token survives.
3The "Ask AI about this plan" drawer on the slip is the home of the paid AI task. Every answer is a proposal; apply is the traveler's click.
4Trip Cart leaves the sidebar. Checkout is reached from the slip's Finish card. The route stays as the guest fallback until G2.
5My events folds into My plans. A done-for-you engagement is a card on its plan's slip. The money rail is untouched.
6The Trip Card loses its tab shell. One page: frozen plan, live status, the booking-agent drawer, and a typographic hero when no market photo is known.
7The traveler console adopts the one site grammar: coral primary, earn tokens, Fraunces headings, Geist Mono eyebrows. Discover, Experts and checkout render inside the shell.
8Home owns the time axis, amending R-A. The page is what is coming up across every plan, dated rows nearest first, plus what changed since the last visit. No plan card, no counts, no messages: those live on My plans, the slip and Inbox.

1 · What exists today, and why it pulls against the slip

"AI Concierge" is three different things. On the pricing map it is a paid AI task at $2.99. In the code, the concierge page is a one-shot quote funnel pricing three tiers, AI, Expert and Done-for-you, that hands off elsewhere. In Decision 44 it is the booking copilot. Nothing on the slip is called concierge except the ready-made revision card, which is human.

The three-tier chooser exists three times. The modal's finish asks myself / AI / local / occasion. The Finalize modal asks myself / agent / expert / concierge. The concierge page asks AI / expert / full. One question answered in three places is the drift class §18 rule 1 names.

The AI Planner is a dead end after the mint. The chat extracts basics, mints through the one create rail, navigates to the slip, and then has no relationship to that plan. Its conversations carry no trip id. The modal's AI finish opens a different component again.

The two AI escalation cards sit on the wrong surface. "Ask the Concierge" mounts on the dashboard summary card, and "AI Plan Polish" mounts only on the full Trip Card. Since D8 redirects every pre-final plan to the slip, polish is reachable only on a plan that is already final.

The cart is still a second store. The authenticated cart route reads its own table, and the slip's Finish card sends "Go to checkout" there. Decision 39 says the cart is a projection of the plan's items with no second store. Two stores feed one checkout page today.

2 · The AI Concierge: one actor, three appearances

Keyed to plan state, never a sidebar destination.

Build card · exists The one AI action

Draft on an empty plan, Optimize on a non-empty one. Review-first, paid gate, free preview beside it. Keep exactly as Decision 41 ratified.

Slip drawer · needs ruling 1, 3 Ask AI about this plan

Where the $2.99 task lives, charged only on apply. Trip Pass coverage of tasks is promised on the pricing map but has no charge site yet (41f), so the drawer does not claim it. The AI reads the same plancard payload the expert reads live and answers with a proposal that lands like an expert suggestion. Apply is the traveler's click. The pre-mint conversation continues here, one AI thread per plan.

After Finalize · Decision 44 The booking agent

A final plan lives on the Trip Card (D8), so the same drawer mounts there and flips to the copilot vocabulary: researching, ready to buy, flagged, unavailable. "Booked" only with a confirmation in hand. Polish becomes a task; escalate-to-human opens the one expert picker.

The concierge page becomes a door. Its intent form already holds destination, occasion and party size, so under D13 it passes those into the one modal and the tier choice becomes the modal's finish. That is the quick-start retirement from D14, applied once more. The guest claim token survives because the door still captures the lead before sign-in.

3 · The AI Planner splits at the mint

Before the mint it is a door. The chat plus draft panel is the conversational way to fill the modal's five questions. "Create this plan" opens the modal pre-filled through the door table, so the traveler confirms stops, dates, party and events on the same steps every other door uses. Today it mints directly and skips the modal.

The mint is the boundary. After it, the same conversation attaches to the trip and renders as the slip drawer. This is what ruling 1 is for.

The sidebar tab stays, renamed to what it is. "Start with AI" is the door for someone with no plan. A conversation that already has a trip opens that trip's slip. The tab never edits an existing plan.

Every AI write is a proposal. Free draft on empty, Optimize or a paid task on non-empty, apply on confirm, expert work protected per D3. No fourth AI write path. This is the posture R-J ratified for connected agents: agents build and stage, humans pay.

The concierge Expert tier posts an expert request with no trip id, the lead-with-no-plan shape Decision 32 closed for template inquiries.

Decision 42 D9, the bookings section on the slip, has no client half. The balance-payer predicate exists server-side only.

Three docs are stale: the trip slip spec still describes the old flat action row, the pricing map §7 lists the Finalize popup and Trip Pass as unbuilt, and the slip canvas cited as ratified authority is not committed anywhere in the repo.

8 · Console styling: one grammar, not two

The site has a grammar and the console half-uses it. The public chrome, landing, expert cards and the slip use Fraunces for headings, Geist Mono for eyebrows and counts, Inter for body, and the earn tokens. The console sidebar hardcodes a parallel warm set, and the page bodies are not console-scoped, so every shadcn primary inside them resolves to the traveler pink rather than coral. Two reds on one screen.

The boards draw one grammar. A masthead per tab in the pattern the Experts page already uses, cards on the earn border and radius, coral for the one primary action, navy for secondary emphasis, teal for the AI and expert washes. The dark TravelPulse card becomes a white card with a teal eyebrow. The mockup's navy-dark aesthetic is retired.

9 · Per-tab enhancements, each traced to a defect or a ruling

Home. Owns the time axis: what is coming up across every plan as dated rows, nearest first, each linking to where it is acted on, plus a since-you-were-here strip with inline actions and a home-city block that renders only when a home city is set. The plan card, the routing counts and messages are not repeated here. Both create tiles open the one planner.

My plans. Show all works and the past list is not capped at three. Message only when an advisor exists.

Start with AI. Continue in the planner replaces Create this plan. A conversation bound to a plan opens that plan's slip.

Discover and Experts. Inside the shell, with an adding-to or hiring-for plan chip. No plan means the chip says so and the action opens the planner.

Bookings. Grouped by plan; every row names the service and the provider. A balance is paid on the slip and only noted here.

Inbox. Each thread carries its context kind from the server. AI threads never appear.

Profile. Payment methods in Stripe's vault; the Occasions card stays unrendered until Plus sales are on.

10 · Build sequence: eighteen lanes in four waves

A wave is a sequence, not a batch: each lane lands serially, one PR at a time, and appends its own ledger row. Wave 0 ratifies and preserves. Wave 1 needs no ruling beyond the record and no schema, so it can start now. Wave 2 waits on its ruling and on the lane it is blocked by. Wave 3 is the one migration and the one lane large enough to need its own brief before it is built. Two things are deliberately not lanes: the push channel for the phone, which is a product decision nobody has taken, and the checkout page's move onto the plan projection, which is a gate inside L7 rather than a lane of its own until the audit says how far it got.
