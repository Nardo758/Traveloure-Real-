## 1. DEFINE — critical-to-quality requirements

The lens is the ratified three-column frame from `docs/briefs/SERVICE_FUNDAMENTALS_DECISIONS.md`:
**client logistics** (can the buyer receive it?) · **provider back office** (can the seller operate it?) ·
**provider marketing** (can the seller sell it?). A gap in the client-logistics column is a *fundamental*
gap — a sold service the platform cannot fulfil.

Archetypes in scope (Kyoto market): **kimono rental/dressing · photo tours · private guides &
interpreters · airport/hotel transfer operators · tea-ceremony hosts · small tour operators.**

| # | CTQ requirement | Lens | Why it is critical for these archetypes |
|---|---|---|---|
| CTQ-1 | The buyer knows **where and when to physically show up**, exactly | client logistics | Every archetype except a PDF is a rendezvous. A wrong meeting point is a total service failure. |
| CTQ-2 | The buyer knows the **cancellation / reschedule terms** before paying, and the seller knows what they signed up to | client logistics + back office | Money-adjacent; the trade runs on tiered day-based windows (§3). |
| CTQ-3 | The buyer knows the **booking lead time / cutoff** | client logistics | Transfers and kimono fittings are capacity-bound; a same-day request the seller cannot serve is a guaranteed failure. |
| CTQ-4 | **Pickup coverage** is expressible as the seller actually operates it (a zone *or* a fixed stop list) | client logistics | Ratified by ruling 62's amendment; matches real operator practice (§3). |
| CTQ-5 | **Party-size bounds** are expressible and enforced | client logistics + back office | Group size changes price, vehicle, and cutoff in this trade (§3). |
| CTQ-6 | The **language the service is delivered in** is declarable and visible | client logistics + marketing | Kyoto is an inbound market; delivery language is a purchasable attribute (§3). |
| CTQ-7 | The seller can **publish without leaving the product**, and a blocked publish states its reason and its remedy | back office | The funnel's terminal step. |
| CTQ-8 | An **edit to a live listing does not silently remove it from the market** | back office | Routine maintenance (price, seasonal note) must not be destructive. |
| CTQ-9 | A sold **artifact is actually deliverable** through the UI | client logistics | D3/R4 ratified the rail; a rail with no control is not a rail. |
| CTQ-10 | Claims a seller makes are **legally safe in the market** (protected professional titles) | back office | Japan-specific and real (§3). |

## 3. External research — how these businesses actually operate

Sources are public operator/aggregator pages and industry references, consulted 2026-08-11. **Where a
question could not be settled, that is said so.** These describe *the trade*, not this platform's users.

**R-1 — Japan deregulated paid guiding in 2018, but the professional TITLE is still protected.**
Since 4 January 2018 a national licence is no longer required to guide for pay; the licence retains one
legal protection — only certified guides may use 通訳案内士 (*Tsūyaku Annai-shi*), and unlicensed guides
are prohibited from titles implying official certification such as "Government certified guide".
Sources: [JNTO — How to book a certified tour guide](https://www.japan.travel/en/plan/how-to-book-a-certified-tour-guide/),
[Hinomaru — licensed vs unlicensed guides](https://hinomaru.one/licensed-vs-unlicensed-tour-guides-what-matters),
[Jasumo — becoming a tour guide in Japan](https://jasumo.com/how-to-become-a-tour-guide-in-japan-a-complete-2025-and-beyond-guide/).
**Consequence for this platform:** the listing title/description are free text with no attestation, so a
provider can type "Government-certified guide" into a Kyoto listing today with nothing asking whether it
is true. This is exactly the surface **D9** (ruling 62) was ratified to cover — attestations keyed to
method + category risk, inapplicable ones omitted with a reason. It is *unbuilt*, and this is a concrete,
market-specific, legally-loaded reason to build it. **Not a licensing gate — a title-claim attestation.**

**R-2 — cancellation windows in this market are tiered and measured in DAYS, and they vary widely.**
Measured examples: wargo free cancellation to 2 days before; Yumeyakata free to the day before, 100% on
the day; Sakura Kimono full refund at 10 days, 50% at 9–5 days, 70–100% at 4 days–same day; Okamoto a flat
¥3,300 same-day fee; Kyolan free until 18:00 three days before, with a **stricter deadline for groups over
10**. Photo tours cluster at 24h/72h/96h with explicit **rain-reschedule** clauses.
Sources: [wargo](https://kyotokimonorental.com/en), [Yumeyakata](https://www.en-kyoto.yumeyakata.com/),
[Sakura Kimono](https://sakura-kimono-kyoto.com/en/kimono-rental-guide/),
[Okamoto FAQ](https://www.okamoto-kimono-en.com/faq/), [Kyolan terms](https://en.kyotorentalkimono.com/notice),
[Kyoto photography tour](https://happytovisit.com/kyoto-photography-tour/).
**Consequence:** the platform's four fixed tiers (24h / 5d+2d / 7d / none) are a reasonable *spine* but
cannot express a same-day flat fee, a rain clause, or a group-size-dependent deadline. The free-text
"details" field is where those must live — which makes M-1 (the seller not seeing the enforced windows)
worse, because the seller may believe the free text governs. **M-1 is now fixed; the expressiveness gap is
filed as Tier B.**

**R-3 — pickup is a *list of named points*, not only a radius, and the "if your hotel isn't listed" case is standard.**
Operators offer hotel-front pickup, or a **selectable list of included pickup points** at checkout, with a
central landmark (commonly Kyoto Station) as the documented fallback when a hotel is not on the list;
riders are asked to be at the door ~10 minutes early.
Sources: [Viator — Kyoto/Nara private tour w/ hotel pickup](https://www.viator.com/tours/Kyoto/Kyoto-Nara-Private-tour-w-Hotel-pick-up-and-Drop-off-from-Kyoto/d332-407697P2),
[GetYourGuide — Kyoto private tours](https://www.getyourguide.com/kyoto-l96826/private-tours-tc221/),
[My Japan Guide](https://myjapanguide.com/tours/kyoto/).
**Consequence:** ruling 62's radius-or-route amendment is **well aimed** — `service_route_points` is
precisely the "list of named pickup points" shape the trade uses, and it measured green (§2.4). The gap
research exposes is the **fallback**: no field expresses "if your hotel isn't on the list, meet me at X",
which every operator states explicitly.

**R-4 — transfers are governed by a booking cutoff, a waiting/grace window, and a passenger+luggage count.**
Typical: bookings to **24h before**, flight changes notified ≥24h, **90 minutes free waiting** at airport
arrivals then an hourly overtime fee, no refund on an unreachable no-show; luggage capacity stated per
vehicle class and the operator requires the exact passenger / child-seat / luggage counts at booking.
Sources: [KKday — KIX private transfer](https://www.kkday.com/en/product/129909-japan-kansai-international-airport-private-transfer-to-osaka-kyoto-nara-kobe-nagoya),
[GetYourGuide — KIX ⇄ Kyoto private transfer](https://www.getyourguide.com/kyoto-l96826/kansai-intl-airport-kix-private-transfer-tofrom-kyoto-t552621/).
**Consequence:** the D7 capture set covers cutoff (`changeCutoffHours`) and party size
(`partySizeMin/Max`) — good aim. It has **no luggage/vehicle-capacity concept and no waiting/grace
window**, and party size is capture-only (ruling 64's declared negative space), so nothing is enforced at
booking. F-1 additionally means the *lead time* a transfer operator types is not the one the system uses.

**R-5 — delivery language is a purchasable attribute, not chrome.**
Shared tea-ceremony sessions are commonly conducted in Japanese, with English requiring a **private
session, sometimes at an added interpreter fee**; operators advertise English-speaking hosts as a
differentiator.
Sources: [byFood — Kyoto tea ceremony](https://www.byfood.com/kyoto-tea-ceremony),
[MAIKOYA](https://mai-ko.com/culture/tea-ceremony/), [Japan Experience — private tea ceremony](https://www.japan-experience.com/activities/kyoto/tea-ceremony-in-kyoto-private).
**Consequence:** this is **distinct from ruling 60**, which ratified (A) UI chrome translation and
(B) provider *content* translation. Neither is "what language is the experience delivered in". F-4 shows
providers have no field for it at all. For an inbound Kyoto market this is a first-order client-logistics
fact.

**Inconclusive / not established by this research:** typical *platform* commission expectations for Kyoto
operators; whether Japanese operators prefer per-person or per-group pricing at the margin; and any
no-show rate. No number was invented to fill these.

## 8. What this pass could NOT measure (and why)

1. **Anything about real users.** Provider count ≈ 0. Nothing here supports a claim about behaviour,
   preference, drop-off, or frequency. The "frequency" column in §4.3 is *journey structure*, not usage.
2. **The signup → approval funnel end to end.** `/signup` assigns role `user` with no role choice
   (measured), so becoming a provider requires separately finding `/become-provider`. This pass measured
   the signup form and then used an **already-approved** provider account; it did not drive a fresh
   account through business verification, which needs real KYB/Stripe-Connect flows unavailable here.
3. **The buyer's actual download.** Journey B seeded a `confirmed` booking and proved the entitlement
   *negatives* (anonymous `401`, non-buyer owner `404`) plus a real upload/stream through the memory
   driver — but could not log in as the buyer (password unknown for that seeded account), so the
   positive download leg rests on `deliverable-protected-rail.http`'s 13 proofs, not on a fresh UI run.
4. **Anything downstream of a real payment.** Stripe key is a local stub; no charge, refund, or payout was
   exercised. All money paths were left strictly alone by design.
5. **Instagram publish round-trip** — externally gated, unchanged from the punchlist's standing entry.
6. **Production data.** Every count is against a synthetic local dev DB (3 listings on the measured
   account). The `#PS17` shared-dev-bench limitation still applies: counts prove the *query*, not the
   population.
7. **Research limits.** §3 describes *the trade* from public operator pages; it is not a survey, has no
   sampling frame, and Kyoto operators are not this platform's providers. Three questions were left
   explicitly **inconclusive** in §3 rather than answered by guess.
8. **Two discarded measurements**, recorded for auditability:
   - A first run reported *"NEVER-CLOBBER VIOLATED — route stops 3 → 0"*. **False.** The harness had
     posted `{points:[…]}` with string coordinates; the endpoint's allowlist is `{stops:[…]}` with
     numbers, so it returned `400` and no stops were ever created. Fixed by adding an explicit
     precondition assertion; the corrected run measured **green** (§2.4). **Ruling 64's rule holds.**
   - A first run reported `/provider/new-service` as the service wizard and recorded a wizard failure.
     **Harness error** — that path renders provider registration; the wizard is `/provider/services/new`
     (this became F-2, correctly scoped as an orphan route with zero inbound links, not a broken wizard).
